# Task 3a Step 6 — reviewed statistical diagnostics design

**Status:** independently reviewed design for the operator-authorized Step 6 continuation; implementation follows a separately reviewed plan. No real-data, broker or inference enablement. **Date:** 2026-09-26. **Owner:** operator.
**Intent:** tell the operator when the signal test has evidence of benchmark-relative edge and when it only has a large, dependent, or incomplete collection of trades. A signal result is diagnostic, not a strategy-promotion verdict.

## 1. Scope and current boundary

Step 6a may build pure estimators and calibrate them on synthetic outcomes. Step 6b may date and pin a calibration-derived inference floor. Neither step evaluates the real panel, touches the 2023-onward lockbox, enables orders, or treats a daily-bar benchmark as an intraday Nifty match. The current simulator emits no benchmark matches and its optional fragment `benchmark_return` is an opaque number, not proof of observed price, timestamp, or artifact. Therefore a non-`None` `SignalTrade.pre_tax_alpha` alone cannot unlock official alpha inference. The separately approved [time-resolved design](2026-09-26-time-resolved-benchmark-design.md) governs that future input.

The existing `signal_test` settings remain pinned: uniform ₹1,00,000 notional; costs on, capital-gains tax off; 95% confidence; block length `L = max(63, 3 × longest_holding_sessions)`; 4,999 placebo draws; seed `20260924`; and `inference_enabled: false`. Step 6 does not revise the promotion gate's 100-trade floor or the stagnation halt. The open delivery-DP-fee/stale-mark discrepancy (audit F48) blocks any claim that current net returns are contract-note exact.

## 2. Alternatives and decision proposed

| Candidate | Benefit | Failure/cost | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary trade-level or entry-day-only interval | Simple | Treats overlapping market exposures and clustered entry bursts as independent | Reject |
| Block-cluster CR1 with `t(occupied_blocks − 1)` | Small implementation | A few high-volume blocks can dominate despite an apparently adequate block count | Retain only as a labelled calibration comparator; not the reporting default |
| Intercept-only, entry-session block-cluster CR2 with Satterthwaite degrees of freedom | Corrects finite-cluster leverage under a declared working model; still reports the trade-weighted mean | More mathematics and tests; does not cure dependence across blocks | **Candidate reporting method, conditional on synthetic calibration** |
| Moving/stationary block bootstrap | Can represent temporal dependence | Prior Icarus synthetic work found essentially the same coverage at much higher cost; many tuning choices | Do not add unless CR2 fails calibration and a new design is reviewed |

The CR2/Satterthwaite choice follows [Pustejovsky and Tipton's small-sample work](https://jepusto.com/publications/RVE-in-fixed-effects-models/index.html) and its [author-maintained CR2 reference](https://jepusto.github.io/clubSandwich/reference/vcovCR.html). This is a candidate to test, not a claim that the literature validates Icarus's particular trade process.

## 3. Observation and time contract

One filled `SignalTrade` contributes one observation to each metric; partial exit fragments are aggregated first. Skips are counted in the denominator reconciliation but are not zero-return trades. A stable `SignalId` and source-axis indices prove uniqueness. Duplicate identities, non-finite returns, non-monotone source sessions, or incompatible time precision are schema refusals, not dropped rows.

The initial outcome surface has separate raw-return, win-rate, benchmark-excess, and placebo components. Raw trade return and per-trade win indicator may be described from existing synthetic `SignalTrade` values, with model-charge limitations visible. A benchmark-excess observation requires a typed, independently validated match certificate covering the entry and every actual exit fragment, not merely the existing Decimal field. A stale residual mark or any missing/ambiguous leg makes that trade's exact excess return unavailable. The certificate names benchmark price-index version, observed source-artifact identity/hash, actual and available event times, matching-rule version, and refusal provenance. The future M15 adapter must keep daily strategy-session indices separate from execution-event coordinates; Step 6 must not feed M15 bars into the daily simulator.

**Official whole-cohort benchmark inference requires 100% valid matches for all filled signal trades in that run**, including no unresolved stale-mark residual. The denominator is all filled trades, not just convenient matched trades. Any incomplete match yields `MATCHING_INCOMPLETE` or `MISSING_BENCHMARK` for official excess-return inference and the bridge verdict. A matched-subset mean and its coverage may be shown as descriptive only, with no favourable whole-cohort claim or p-value. This conservative floor is proposed because selectively missing intraday outcomes can bias the mean in either direction; it may make official alpha unavailable until source coverage and execution timing improve. The 2011–2022 daily study and separately pinned 2015–2022 M15 timing study remain distinct: the former may report raw signal outcomes but cannot claim exact intraday-exit benchmark alpha. Missing M15 refinement never removes a name from the daily point-in-time universe, while the M15 study's missing matches remain visible in its full-cohort denominator.

## 4. Exact candidate interval contract

For each completed run, fix the canonical daily session axis and its pre-registered study-origin index, even if early sessions have no trades. For a fold-matched run, keep the same full-source origin, then analyse only that fold's observations; do not re-anchor at its first trade. For the separate M15 study, use its own pinned 2015-start daily-session origin. Define `H` as the greatest observed `holding_sessions` among that run's filled trades, including stale recognition duration; `L = max(63, 3H)`. Assign trade `i` to block `floor((entry_session_index_i − study_origin_index) / L)`. Report `H`, `L`, the origin, occupied block count, and each block's trade count. Empty blocks are retained in the time-axis provenance but do not create degrees of freedom. Edge blocks are not merged or re-anchored. Recompute the declared `H → L` rule for each full or fold run, and report when the resulting `L` differs.

For a metric vector `y_i` with `N` trades and mean `m = Σy_i/N`, let occupied block `g` contain `n_g` trades, `h_g=n_g/N`, and residual sum `S_g=Σ(i in g)(y_i−m)`. Under an intercept-only working model with independent, equal-variance trade errors for the **correction only**, the CR2 variance candidate is `V = Σ_g S_g²/[N²(1−h_g)]`. Refuse if `N<2`, fewer than two occupied blocks, any `h_g≥1`, non-finite arithmetic, or `V≤0`. This working model is not asserted to describe real trades; observed within-block dependence is represented by the block score, and cross-block dependence is tested in calibration.

Set `K_gh = [1(g=h)n_g − n_g n_h/N] / [N² sqrt((1−h_g)(1−h_h))]`. The Satterthwaite degrees of freedom are `ν = tr(K)² / tr(K²)`. Refuse a non-positive or non-finite `ν`; the minimum acceptable occupied blocks **and** minimum `ν` are calibrated and pinned before inference. The proposed two-sided interval is `m ± t_(ν,0.975) sqrt(V)`, using the already pinned 95% level. For win rate, `y_i=1` only when that trade's net return is strictly positive, otherwise 0; apply the same metric-specific calculation and clip displayed interval endpoints to `[0,1]`. A zero-variance all-win/all-loss set has a descriptive win rate but no interval. Numeric implementation must be checked against an independent CR2/Satterthwaite oracle on hand and synthetic examples; no R runtime dependency is added to Icarus.

Report raw trade count `N`, occupied blocks, `ν`, sample variance `s²` (denominator `N−1`), design effect `V/(s²/N)`, and variance-equivalent effective sample size `s²/V` separately for each metric. If `s²` or `V` is invalid/zero, the last two are unavailable. The displayed effective sample size is capped at `N`, with the uncapped value and cap flag retained for audit; it is a variance-equivalent diagnostic, not an independent trade count or a new promotion floor. Win rate never borrows return's effective sample size. Its synthetic validation uses known win-probability targets across an interior and near-boundary grid; a zero-mean return test is not a win-rate null, and clipping the displayed endpoints does not repair bad coverage. Report medians/quantiles as descriptive; do not attach this mean interval to them.

## 5. Calibration and enablement gate

Before Step 6a simulation, freeze the synthetic calibration and refusal matrices and candidate floors in a reviewed implementation plan. Stochastic coverage profiles include independent trades, shared market factor, same-day/time-bursty entries, unequal occupancy, long overlapping holds and edge crossings, bounded cross-block serial dependence, bounded heavy tails, and mean/regime shifts. Sparse/no-trade, deterministic all-win/all-loss, duplicate and non-finite inputs belong in the required-refusal matrix unless a separate stochastic profile with known targets is declared. Calibration and untouched validation seeds/fixtures are distinct from the reporting permutation seed. Do not tune on the validation half.

Pre-register **two different matrices**. The coverage matrix uses stochastic profiles with known return mean and known win probability, within bounded/explicit dependence, tail, horizon and fold-length envelopes; its return-mean and win-rate intervals are judged against their own targets. The required-refusal matrix contains empty, duplicate, non-finite, one-block, invalid-benchmark and observable out-of-envelope cases; it is not included in empirical coverage denominators. Refusals are **component-specific**: differing positive returns may yield a valid return interval while their all-win indicator yields `ZERO_VARIANCE` for win-rate inference. An all-loss indicator behaves the same way. A refused win-rate component does not erase a valid return component. Count every generated, emitted and refused replicate by reason. A coverage cell cannot pass by refusing most runs: the implementation plan must pin a minimum emission fraction and absolute emitted count before simulation, as well as replicate counts, coverage/false-positive tolerances, and uncertainty bounds on those rates. A finite block floor is not a guarantee under arbitrary cross-block dependence; failed bounded profiles keep inference off. Runtime envelope refusals apply only to observable features such as span, holding duration, block occupancy and source precision. Latent dependence and tail parameters cannot be reliably certified from one finite sample and remain prominently stated limitations, never claimed as detected guarantees.

Before simulation, freeze an **ordered list of `(minimum occupied blocks, minimum ν)` candidate pairs** and a first-passing selection rule. One pair must satisfy the return, win-rate and synthetic excess-return coverage cells, then pass every untouched validation cell; pairs are not chosen separately for whichever metric looks best. If the first pair that passes calibration fails **any** held-back validation cell, inference stays off and the design returns for review; do not try the next pair on that validation set. If no pair passes calibration, inference also stays off. Do not omit failed profiles or choose the interval that flatters a strategy. Synthetic fixtures are not real-data strategy trials. Step 6b records the chosen minimum block and `ν` floors with dated, append-only provenance in config and tests loader refusal of an unlogged change.

Keep the existing `inference_enabled` master kill **false through Step 6b and all synthetic-only work**. Step 6b pins the calibrated floors but does not unlock output. A later dated and separately reviewed change may allow the master flag only after the Step 7 ledger, time matcher, F48 cost correction, and source-specific matching rule pass. Even then, each typed component independently requires its own valid inputs and emits a refusal when unavailable; a master true is never evidence that the benchmark or placebo component is ready. No new per-component config booleans are needed. This is a proposed Task 3a sequencing amendment, not permission to change config today.

## 6. Fixed-footprint placebo: descriptive until exchangeability is proven

The placebo asks whether **symbol selection** beat a same-footprint random comparison, not whether a full alternate strategy would have traded. **Step 6a builds only its typed result/refusal boundary and synthetic exchangeability checks, not a replacement-price engine.** For each observed entry session, draw from names in the daily point-in-time eligible/tradable universe known at decision time. Exclude that session's actually selected names, sample without replacement within that session, allow reuse across different sessions, and process signals in sorted `SignalId` order with the pinned seed. A later separately reviewed replacement-price design must choose either hypothetical fractional exposure (exactly fixed fragment weights) or integer-share rounding (possibly changed fragment weights), define its estimand and cost convention, and price names with the same declared M15 event/matching rules. It must not pretend the replacement had its own stops/targets. Until that choice and the source path are approved, real placebo returns and p-values are unavailable. The observed statistic `T_observed` is the mean net-of-modelled-charges return of all filled signal trades in the run; each replacement statistic `T_draw` is the mean replacement net return over that identical fixed footprint. An undersized point-in-time replacement pool has an `INSUFFICIENT_REFERENCE_UNIVERSE` refusal. A sampled name with an unknown/no-fill/ambiguous required event, missing price, or an unauditable cost does not get swapped for a more convenient name: refuse that draw and the placebo component, with its reason. Never condition the replacement universe on later survival or successful exit data.

The current data and design do not establish exchangeability of selected and random names (liquidity, price, sector, and availability can differ). Therefore real-data placebo **p-values stay unavailable** until a separately reviewed, pre-registered assignment/stratification argument and complete-footprint source path exist. Synthetic fixtures with truly exchangeable labels may exercise the machinery. If that later gate passes, use all 4,999 draws and the upper-tail, tie-inclusive plus-one value `p = [1 + #{T_draw ≥ T_observed}]/5000`; `p` is never zero. A refused placebo does not erase a valid, independently matched alpha interval. [Phipson and Smyth](https://gksmyth.github.io/pubs/PermPValuesPreprint.pdf) support the Monte Carlo correction, not exchangeability.

## 7. Typed outputs, refusals, and bridge

Each component is either available with its observation count, method/provenance and bounds, or unavailable with a typed reason. At minimum: `EMPTY_SAMPLE`, `MISSING_BENCHMARK`, `ZERO_VARIANCE`, `TOO_FEW_BLOCKS`, `INDEFENSIBLE_PLACEBO_NULL`, and `MATCHING_INCOMPLETE`; temporal ambiguity, stale mark, bad provenance, invalid schema, and cost-inexactness are distinct subreasons. A component refusal never turns a missing return into zero or suppresses valid raw trade outcomes. No `Sharpe`, equity curve, drawdown, CAGR, or generic gate alpha appears on a signal result.

Treat benchmark evidence and portfolio comparison as **orthogonal typed states**, not one incomplete three-way enum. Official whole-cohort excess-return interval upper bound `≤0` gives `NO_EDGE`; an interval spanning zero or unavailable gives `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`; lower bound `>0` gives `POSITIVE_PRETAX_EXCESS_EVIDENCE`. Portfolio comparison is separately `FAILED`, `PASSED`, or `UNAVAILABLE` on exactly matched folds. Only positive excess evidence plus a failed portfolio comparison may additionally be labelled `POSSIBLE_EDGE_WRONG_BOOK`; positive excess with a passing or unavailable portfolio retains its positive diagnostic evidence without that label or any promotion claim. These states never emit `PROMOTED`, change a threshold, or infer that portfolio constraints caused the difference. The separately required after-cost-and-tax promotion gate remains authoritative.
